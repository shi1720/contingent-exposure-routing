Model failover can preserve service availability while concentrating financial decision errors. We study outage-contingent assignment of model endpoints to institutions through a local market-impact response matrix. A sharp construction shows that common fallback leaves a nonzero exposure-concentration floor when a fixed fraction of endpoints fails, whereas balanced fallback risk decays with the surviving endpoint count. For indivisible decisions, we derive the exact second moment of independent randomized routing and an effective-exposure granularity that controls its gap from fractional allocation. We give a conditional-expectation assignment bound and a separate quota-preserving implementation with dual lower bounds. Across 60 synthetic portfolio networks and 11,340 scenario evaluations, exposure-aware reassignment reduces quadratic price-displacement risk by 6.57% and 10.53% for single and double removals at the central independent-error setting, with unchanged endpoint counts. A held-out replay of recorded model errors gives a smaller 3.30% reduction. Common errors and unequal model quality limit diversification. The results provide a reproducible contingency-routing stress test, not an estimate of market crash probabilities.
